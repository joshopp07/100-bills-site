The President For the People Amendment

The President For the People Amendment makes the President’s oath mean that the President carries out the office within the law, and no power of the office includes permission to commit a federal crime. A President who breaks a federal criminal law while in office answers for it after leaving office, as any other person would, whether or not Congress has impeached. Calling the crime an official act does not excuse it. Selling a pardon, or using any power of the office through criminal means or for a corrupt purpose, remains a crime. The time limit for charging those crimes pauses while the person holds office, and no President can self-pardon.

  • No former President is above federal criminal law: a crime committed in office carries the same consequences it would carry for anyone else.
  • Calling a crime an official act, or an exercise of the President’s powers, no longer excuses it.
  • The choice of whom to pardon or whom to remove stays the President’s to make, but a pardon sold for money, or a power used through criminal means or for a corrupt purpose, remains a crime.
  • No President can self-pardon, or hand power to a stand-in to issue that pardon, and any such pardon is void.
  • The time limit for charging a federal crime stops running while the person holds the office, so a President cannot wait out the clock.
