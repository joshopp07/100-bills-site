The Public Land Stays Public Act

The public domain belongs to the public. Under the Public Land Stays Public Act, a national monument can be abolished or shrunk only by an Act of Congress, and the land a past presidential order cut from one goes back inside its boundaries. Ocean waters a president has closed to offshore leasing stay closed unless Congress reopens them. Selling more than 2,500 acres of public land takes an Act of Congress too. Hardrock mines pay a new fee, and cleanup of abandoned mines has permanent funding. About 8.3 million acres of public land in the West sit behind property corners you could step across.

  • You can step across the corner where two parcels of public land meet, on foot with your pack or your game, without trespassing.
  • The government buys access to public land that has no public way in, from willing sellers only.
  • A land swap cannot close until every appraisal behind it has been public for a set period, and the public has to get land of equal or greater value.
  • Fishing catch limits allow no exceptions beyond the ones Congress wrote, and owners of small fishing boats do not pay for the catch monitoring the bill requires.
  • Ranchers grazing cattle on public land pay a fee tied to what private pasture rents for in the West, reached in equal yearly steps.
