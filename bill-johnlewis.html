The John R. Lewis Voting Rights Act

The John R. Lewis Voting Rights Act lets you vote by mail in any federal election without giving a reason, finding a witness, or paying for a stamp. It protects you from voting rules that leave voters of your race with less chance to vote, even where nobody can prove the rule was meant to do that, and from congressional maps drawn to make your vote count for less. It keeps your voter record away from immigration enforcement and stops a presidential order or agency directive from changing the rules of your election in the months before voting begins.

  • You can vote by mail in any federal election without an excuse, a witness, or a notary, sign up once to get a mail ballot for later federal elections, and send it back with prepaid postage or leave it at a secure drop box, with at least one in every county.
  • If your mail ballot has a problem with its signature, its date, or its envelope, you are told and given a chance to fix it before it can be thrown out.
  • A congressional map cannot be drawn to make the votes of a party’s supporters or of a racial group count for less, and a state cannot defend a rigged map by admitting it was drawn to hurt one instead of the other.
  • From 90 days before voting begins until the results are certified, the federal government cannot send agents, armed or not, to your polling place, a drop box, or a room where ballots are counted on its own say-so, even to investigate a suspected crime.
  • A state or locality with a recent record of voting rights violations needs federal approval before making it harder to vote.
