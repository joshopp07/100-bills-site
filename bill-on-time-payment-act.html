The On-Time Payment Act

The On-Time Payment Act pays Social Security, SSI, veterans’ compensation and pension, federal civilian retirement, and military retired pay twice a month instead of once. Nothing changes about who qualifies or how much anyone receives, and there is nothing to sign up for. The first half comes on the day you are paid now and the second about fifteen days later, so the longest wait between payments shrinks from about a month to about two weeks. The switch begins with one early half-payment, the first half of the next month’s check arriving about two weeks ahead, so no one waits longer than today and no one receives more over the year.

  • Your payment date does not move: the first half arrives on the day your benefit comes now, and the second about fifteen days later.
  • In the first month of the change you receive your full usual payment on your usual date, plus the first half of the next month’s benefit about fifteen days later.
  • The federal government has to pay small businesses it contracts with within 15 days.
  • A large company that pays a small business late owes it interest automatically, at a rate tied to what borrowing costs a small firm, without the small business having to ask.
