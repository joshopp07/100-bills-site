The Real Employer Act

Misclassifying a worker saves a business an estimated 20 to 40 percent of its labor costs, and a company that plays by the rules loses out to one that does not. The Real Employer Act narrows that gap by giving people who work as contractors, or through staffing agencies, franchises, and subcontractors, a pay floor and paid sick time. If a business has used your work in at least six of the last 12 months and paid you at least $2,000, every hour you work counts, and the company cannot push the job’s costs onto you without paying for them. The bill does not change whether you are an employee or a contractor.

  • However you are paid, by the job, the delivery, or the hour, your total for each pay period has to reach the pay floor for every hour you worked, and any shortfall is owed to you on the last day of that period.
  • Time the company controls between assignments, including travel from one job to the next, is paid at the pay floor, though your commute to the first job and home from the last is not.
  • You earn paid sick time as you work, usable for your own illness or a family member’s, for medical care, or when domestic violence, sexual assault, or stalking forces you to miss work, and no one can make you disclose a diagnosis to get paid.
  • If a job requires you to bring a car, tools, or supplies, the company has to provide them, state a price for them up front, or owe you what they cost.
  • No one can fire you, cut your work, or shut you out of an app for asking about your pay, claiming what you are owed, or reporting a violation.
