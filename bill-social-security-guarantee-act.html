The Social Security Guarantee Act

Social Security’s retirement trust fund is projected to run out in late 2032. After that, incoming taxes cover only about 78 percent of benefits, and today’s law does not say how the rest is paid. The Social Security Guarantee Act guarantees that every benefit is paid in full after the trust fund runs out, ending the uncertainty over what retirees will receive. It gives every retiree a raise, largest as a share of the check for retirees with modest lifetime earnings. Someone who retires at full retirement age after 30 years of work gets a benefit above the poverty line. The cap on wages taxed for Social Security ends, so every dollar of wages is taxed.

  • Benefits are paid in full after the retirement trust fund runs out, instead of only as far as incoming taxes reach.
  • Every retiree, including people already retired, gets a raise, largest as a share of the check for people who earned less over their working lives.
  • Yearly cost-of-living increases follow a price index built on what older Americans buy.
  • Anyone who retires at full retirement age after 30 years of work gets a benefit above the poverty line.
  • Every dollar of wages and self-employment income is subject to Social Security tax, with no cap, and a business owner’s share of the profit is taxed the way wages are, so paying yourself a low salary does not avoid it.
