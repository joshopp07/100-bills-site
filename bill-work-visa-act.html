The Work Visa Act

The Work Visa Act brings in workers from abroad when the economy needs them, on terms that protect the paychecks of people already working here. The number of work visas follows the job market, and every visa worker is paid at least what the job pays here and can quit for another sponsoring employer. Today’s farm visa covers only temporary or seasonal work, so dairies and hog and poultry farms get a lawful way to hire for jobs that last all year. Employers who underpay owe a multiple of the wages they kept, and the federal hiring check updates a new citizen’s record automatically.

  • An employer cannot hire a visa worker for less than it pays you and your coworkers in the same job, or for less than the local starting rate where it has no one else in that job, counting benefits as well as wages.
  • A worker on a work visa can quit and take a job with another employer that sponsors work visas.
  • The number of work visas rises when jobs go unfilled and falls when unemployment rises.
  • Dairies and hog and poultry farms can hire workers from abroad lawfully for jobs that last all year.
  • A worker who reports an employer for breaking the law is protected from deportation while the report is checked, nothing in an honest report can be used against them, and a confirmed report can earn legal work status.
