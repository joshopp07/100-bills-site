The Ending Surprise Ambulance Bills and Fake ERs Act

The Ending Surprise Ambulance Bills and Fake ERs Act protects people at each step of an emergency, from the ambulance to the emergency room to the hospital that can treat them. A ground ambulance cannot bill beyond a capped share, whoever runs it. Any healthcare facility outside the federal and Tribal systems that calls itself an emergency room or takes walk-in emergency patients has to examine a patient and stabilize or transfer them before asking about payment, ending a loophole for emergency rooms that don’t accept Medicare. When a local hospital lacks the bed or specialists a patient needs, their doctor can reach a national line staffed at all hours by a doctor trained in emergency or critical care.

  • A patient’s share of a ground ambulance bill cannot exceed 10 percent of its price and counts toward the deductible and out-of-pocket limit, and the bill has to list what was done, what is owed, and that no other amount can be sought.
  • The ambulance protections apply to every ground ambulance, whether a city, a volunteer squad, a nonprofit, or a company runs it.
  • An emergency room cannot make a patient wait while it asks about insurance or payment, and its duty to examine a patient and to stabilize or transfer them does not depend on citizenship or immigration status.
  • A national line, staffed at all hours by an emergency or critical-care doctor, gets the treating doctor advice from the right specialist, such as a surgeon when a patient may need an operation, finds a hospital with the bed the patient needs, and says when a patient can appropriately be treated where they are.
  • A decision to turn down a patient’s transfer to another hospital has to be made by a person, named in the record, and information shared to arrange the transfer cannot be used to deny coverage, used for law enforcement or immigration purposes, or sold.
