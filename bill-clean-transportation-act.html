The Clean Transportation Act

The Clean Transportation Act makes cars cheaper to repair and to run, safer for the people around them, and cleaner to drive. New cars and trucks have to meet the greenhouse-gas limits of the repealed 2024 standards, written into law so no administration can weaken them, and bigger vehicles no longer get easier targets. Bumpers absorb low-speed bumps to keep car maintenance and insurance prices down, independent shops get the data to fix your car, and carmakers can sell to you directly. Public chargers post their prices, renters and condo owners can install their own, and airlines have to meet a fuel-efficiency standard for their fleets.

  • Any independent repair shop gets the carmaker’s diagnostic and sensor-calibration data.
  • Every new car, SUV, and pickup carries an impact-absorbing bumper at a common height, built so a low-speed crash does no damage beyond the bumper face, including to cameras, sensors, and lights.
  • New cars, SUVs, and pickups brake automatically for pedestrians and cyclists, their hoods and bumpers are designed to injure less, and front ends can be no taller than a set limit.
  • A carmaker can sell and service its cars directly to you, whatever your State’s dealer laws say.
  • Public chargers post their price per kilowatt-hour and how often they work, and new chargers are built on federal land such as parks, forests, and federal buildings.
