The Data Warrant Act

Under the Data Warrant Act, clicking "I agree" on an app’s terms does not count as letting the government have your data. A declassified 2022 intelligence report found that location data on millions of Americans is openly for sale and that intelligence agencies can buy it, data police would need a warrant to demand from a phone company. If a federal, state, or local agency would need a warrant to demand records about you, it cannot buy or rent them instead. Federal agents can enter your home to arrest or search only with a judge’s warrant, with consent, or in an emergency the courts recognize. An investigation that goes to a judge first can go forward.

  • The requirement for the government to obtain your location history, your browsing and search history, and your messages is a judge’s warrant, whether a company, an agency, or anyone else holds them, and however they were collected.
  • An agency cannot get around these limits by having a contractor, another agency, or a foreign government get your records and pass them along.
  • A record an agency got by breaking these rules cannot be used against you in any court or hearing, and an officer’s good faith does not change that.
  • A warrant an agency signs for itself does not open your door, and that rule is the same whatever the citizenship or immigration status of anyone inside.
  • Anyone gathering news for the public, including a person filming a police stop to post it, has the same protection against naming a confidential source as a reporter with a press badge, and the government cannot sidestep it by going to the phone company.
