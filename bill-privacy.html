The Personal Privacy Act

The Personal Privacy Act stops companies from picking the ads you see from a profile of you. Ads can still follow the page you are reading or the search you typed. By default, a company you deal with can collect and keep only what it needs to give you the product or service you asked for, and it deletes the data once that need ends. Agreeing to a privacy policy does not count as consent, and you can take back any consent as easily as you gave it. You can ask a company to show you the data it holds about you, tell you who received it, correct it, delete it, or send it to a competitor.

  • If your browser or phone sends a privacy signal, every company’s site and app has to honor it, and no consent banner can block the page you came to see.
  • Your health information, precise location, biometric data, private messages, and online activity profile cannot be sold, whatever you agreed to.
  • A company cannot charge you more, give you a lesser version, or shut you out of a rewards program because you said no to a request for data it does not need.
  • A single free request tells every registered data broker to delete data about you and stop collecting more, and a person at risk of violence or stalking can have it done within 72 hours without proving anything.
  • A device’s microphone, camera, and other sensors can collect data about you only while you are using the function they serve, and none of it can be used to sell you ads.
