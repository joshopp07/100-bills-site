The American Influence Act

The American Influence Act rebuilds America’s diplomacy and foreign aid and makes any order dismantling them void. The U.S. Agency for International Development returns as an independent agency, and humanitarian aid gets permanent funding sent in order of need. Diplomats cannot be pushed out for their politics or their lawful advice. A President’s announcement that the United States is leaving a treaty, an international organization, or an agreement Congress approved does not cut off its funding or end its duties under U.S. law. Students and professionals can count on exchange programs such as Fulbright continuing year after year.

  • Humanitarian aid gets a permanent funding floor starting at the level Congress provided to the main humanitarian aid account for 2026, sent in order of how severe the need is, with a reserve for crises no one foresaw.
  • The U.S. Agency for International Development is restored as an independent agency.
  • Members of the Foreign Service cannot be fired, reassigned, or disciplined for their political affiliation or for lawful advice, and those separated from 2025 through 2028 have a one-time right to return.
  • A President’s announcement that the United States is leaving a treaty, an international organization, or an agreement Congress approved does not cut off its funding or end the duties it created under U.S. law.
  • An agreement with other countries on AI safety, biosecurity, climate, or space debris that meets goals Congress sets gets an up-or-down vote in Congress.
