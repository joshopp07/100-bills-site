The No Shutdowns Act

The No Shutdowns Act ends government shutdowns and debt-limit standoffs. If Congress misses a budget deadline, federal workers keep working and getting paid, and families keep getting food aid and other benefits. Other programs keep their last enacted funding, adjusted each year so a standoff cannot shrink them. Members of Congress, not the public, bear the cost of a missed deadline. If interest on the debt is too high compared with the size of the economy, a fix comes to the floor of each chamber with no committee able to block it, and nothing is cut automatically.

  • When Congress misses a budget deadline, federal workers, including air traffic controllers, keep working and getting paid, and families keep getting food aid and other benefits at the rates the law already sets.
  • Members of Congress, the President, and the Vice President, and no other official, have their pay held back when Congress misses a budget deadline, and receive it once a budget passes.
  • Cutting or ending a program takes a law that says so; leaving it out of a budget does not end it.
  • A benefit or service program that Congress authorizes after this law takes effect keeps running when its authorization runs out unless that law expressly says it should end, though tax provisions still expire as written, and a deadline on a government power, such as surveillance, still ends that power on its date.
  • The debt limit always equals what is needed to pay for obligations already enacted by law, so the limit can never force a default.
