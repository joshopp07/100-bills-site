The One Medicare Act

Under the One Medicare Act, everyone living legally in the United States or its territories has Medicare from birth or from the day they qualify, with nothing to apply for. Income changes what a person pays, never whether the person is covered, and no renewal, unpaid bill, or work rule can take that coverage away. A person with coverage through work can keep it, and Medicare takes over the moment it ends. People on Medicaid, CHIP, and marketplace plans move onto Medicare on one date written into the law, with no gap in coverage and nothing to file, and states no longer pay any share of Medicaid’s cost.

  • A person with health coverage through a job can keep it for as long as the job provides it, and the moment that coverage ends, for any reason, Medicare takes over with nothing to file and no gap.
  • Nothing but a person’s own written choice can end Medicare for someone who still qualifies. There are no renewals to miss and no work requirement, and an unpaid premium is collected as a debt, never by cutting off coverage.
  • What a person pays for Medicare follows one income scale: nothing at the lowest incomes, then a smooth rise with no jump at age 65 and none from one more dollar of income. An adult gets this income-based help once the adult asks for a check of citizenship or lawful presence and the check confirms it. The request can be made in any form at any time, and the help reaches back to the month of asking. During a pregnancy and the 60 days after it, the help applies with no check.
  • Medicare charges no late-enrollment penalty, a person can turn it down and take it back at any time, and coverage based on a disability starts without the two-year wait.
  • Anyone on Medicare, Medicaid, or CHIP when the changeover comes is protected from paying more in premiums and out-of-pocket costs than the old rules would have charged at the same income, and gets a yearly statement showing both amounts. Each adult on Medicare, Medicaid, or CHIP at the changeover is treated as having asked, on the changeover date, for the check of citizenship or lawful presence, except an adult whom Medicaid covered only for emergencies, who must ask. For an adult on Medicare, Medicaid, or CHIP at the changeover, the protection against paying more than the old rules would have charged lasts until the check finishes without confirming citizenship or lawful presence. If the check confirms only a temporary status, that protection ends when the status ends or a year after the check confirmed it, whichever comes first, unless a new check confirms the status again or the status is extended. Even after an adult loses the protection against paying more than the old rules would have charged, the protection still applies to emergency care, and to all care during a pregnancy and the 60 days after it.
