The Child Care and Pre-K Act

The Child Care and Pre-K Act makes early childhood education a right, as open as public school, for every child who lives here from birth until kindergarten, with a provider the parent chooses, whatever the family’s income or jobs. A family earning under three-quarters of its state’s median income pays nothing, and a family above that line pays a share of only the income above it. Three- and four-year-olds get free pre-K for a school day, all school year. Providers in the program cannot add fees on top of the copay, and child care teachers earn at least what local public school teachers with the same credentials and experience earn.

  • Every child who lives here, from birth until kindergarten, is entitled to early education and care with a provider you choose, for as many hours as you choose up to full time, and you do not have to work, be in school, or prove your income to get it.
  • For one child, your copay is never more than 7 percent of what your family earns above three-quarters of your state’s median income, a second child in care at the same time adds no more than 2.5 percentage points, and twins count as one child.
  • A provider in the program cannot charge you a registration fee, deposit, supply fee, or fundraising obligation, and falling behind on your copay can never cost your child a spot.
  • A three- or four-year-old gets free pre-K for a school day, all school year, with care for the rest of the day at your regular copay.
  • If no provider in the program has a place for your child close to home, you can use any eligible caregiver, including a grandparent or aunt, paid by the program, and public money goes to opening new places where they are short.
