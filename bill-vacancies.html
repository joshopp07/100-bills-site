The Confirmed Officials Act

Federal agencies should be run by people the Senate has confirmed, not by stand-ins who stay past their legal time. Under the Confirmed Officials Act, decisions made by an official serving without lawful authority do not count, and no later approval can rescue them. A President cannot swap an expired stand-in for a new one the Senate never approved. Ordinary people do not pay for the government’s mistake: what you received in good faith from such an official cannot be taken back because of it.

  • If an acting Cabinet secretary’s legal time runs out, a Senate-confirmed officer already serving in that department takes over the work for a limited time, and no one else can be put in the job instead.
  • A decision against you by an official serving past the legal limit has no force, no matter how long ago it was made or how hard it would be to redo.
  • A license, benefit, or status you received in good faith from such an official cannot be taken back because that official lacked authority.

Sections of the bill

  1. § 101. Enforcement reaches every vacant office
  2. § 102. Exceeding the time limit voids
  3. § 103. A confirmed officer serves, on a clock
