This is Project 2027-2029: 100 Bills in 100 Days.

Public approval of government and of both parties is at record lows. If Democrats win control of government in 2029, Congress should deliver the biggest wave of reform in history: 100 bills in 100 days.

That means legislation large enough to meet the scale of today's challenges. It means building more of what is scarce, and building it faster. It means a tax code that closes the loopholes the richest use to pay less than the rate the law says. It means light-touch rules that make life more affordable for everyday Americans who are harmed by market failures. And it means a government that pays its bills, delivers what it promises, and re-earns trust by doing both.

Policy made by executive order is policy the next President reverses, so this agenda is written for Congress to pass. A fast start in 2029 means a busy Congress in 2027 and 2028, because a bill that has not been written by then will not pass in time. An agenda that belongs to Congress does not depend on who the nominee is, so it can be built now. This package was written by an emergency room doctor and father of two, with the help of AI tools, to light a fire under the professionals.

Two constitutional amendments sit alongside the hundred bills and are not counted among them: the Supreme Court Terms Amendment, which opens a seat every two years and holds a Justice in active service for eighteen, and the Money in Elections Amendment, which returns to Congress the power to limit election spending. A statute cannot undo a constitutional holding, so where a holding blocks the goal the instrument is an amendment.

The agenda, by theme

How every bill is built

Rules with yes-or-no answers

Is the clause void? Did the custodian file? A rule that needs an agency to exercise discretion well fails fastest under an administration that does not want it to work.

Obligations that run without permission

Filings generate themselves. Publication is a duty a court can compel. The offices these bills create receive, publish, and refer. They decide nothing.

Enforcement by the people affected

A citizen denied information the law entitles them to can sue. Every monetary remedy is set by a court, never by an agency.

Built to survive

Core functions continue through a shutdown. Every major provision is severable, and the boldest protections carry a narrower fallback on settled ground.

The benefit arrives without a form

Enrollment is the default. Eligibility comes from records the government already holds. The person confirms rather than drafts.

Written for the Court we have

Every provision is drafted to survive review under current doctrine, after Trump v. Slaughter, Loper Bright, and West Virginia v. EPA.
