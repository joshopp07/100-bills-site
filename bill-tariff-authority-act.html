The Tariff Authority Act

The Tariff Authority Act gives the decision to tax imports back to Congress. A tariff a President imposes ends after 60 days unless Congress votes to keep it, and tariffs a President has already imposed on most goods face the same clock. A tariff that lapsed cannot be put back on the same goods without a vote. Food and the supplies farmers buy are shielded from any tariff a President imposes without a vote of Congress. The Act itself adds no new tariff on anything.

  • No President can put a tariff on food or on the supplies farmers buy without a vote of Congress.
  • A tariff imposed in the name of national security has to come with a published finding of what it would cost the Americans who buy the goods.
  • A 1930 law that lets a President put tariffs of up to 50 percent on a country’s goods, or bar them entirely, is repealed.
  • The special 25 percent tariff on imported pickup trucks and work vans drops to the 2.5 percent rate that cars pay.
  • Ships built abroad can carry goods between American ports, with American crews under the American flag.
