The Clean Industry Act

The Clean Industry Act pushes American steel, cement, aluminum, fertilizer, glass, and basic chemicals to get cleaner without ordering any plant to close. Each plant is measured against a carbon line built from the reports plants file with the government. At the start, only the dirtiest tenth of production is over the line, which tightens as the cleanest plants improve, plus a small set step. A plant over the line pays a set price for each extra ton of carbon or buys credits from a cleaner plant. Imports over the line pay a charge per excess ton at the border, set at the price an American plant pays if it does not buy credits.

  • No steel, cement, aluminum, fertilizer, glass, or chemical plant is ordered to shut down under the bill, and a plant over the carbon line pays a set price for each extra ton or buys credits instead.
  • In the first year of the rule, only the plants making the dirtiest tenth of each product are over the line, and the line tightens only as the cleanest plants improve, plus a small set step.
  • A plant that beats the line earns a credit for each ton it saves and can sell those credits to plants making the same product that fall short.
  • The price per extra ton rises every year on a schedule written into law, and no official can cut it.
  • Under the American Grid Act, factories can borrow to switch to thermal storage, electric boilers, and industrial heat pumps, with payments no larger than their fuel savings and no credit check.
