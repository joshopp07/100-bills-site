The Government Simplification Act

Federal agencies’ own estimates put the paperwork they require at more than 11 billion hours a year. The Government Simplification Act makes agencies count the time and steps their processes take and publish that count, so you can see the time and documents a federal application will take before you start it. You can say what went wrong on the form, website, or phone line where it happened, and what you say can only help you. This law cannot be used to cut a benefit, narrow who qualifies, raise a fee, or weaken a protection for health, safety, the environment, civil rights, consumers, or workers.

  • Before you begin a federal application or other process, you can look up how long people take to complete it, how long the agency takes to decide, how many steps and documents it requires, how many people give up partway, and how many are denied over paperwork.
  • Federal forms, websites, offices, and phone lines offer a one-step way to report a problem, with no name, account, or terms of use required, even if you never started the process.
  • What you report cannot be used against you or in deciding whether you qualify, though it can be used to restore, speed up, or increase your benefit or to fix an error.
  • A small business seeking a Small Business Administration loan or a small-business contract can authorize the IRS, in the same step as the application, to give the agency the business’s own receipts, payroll, or employee figures, and a figure that matches does not have to be proven again with documents.
  • An agency cannot answer a reported problem by cutting the service, reducing its hours or locations, or moving you to a channel nobody measures.
