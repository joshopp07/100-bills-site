The AI Safety Act

The AI Safety Act requires AI systems above a set size, or showing dangerous abilities, to pass independent safety testing before they can be put to use. Training of still larger systems stops until a developer shows it can contain its models, shut them down, and detect deception. If an AI system causes a catastrophe, or helps someone cause one, the company owes the full cost without anyone having to prove carelessness. A model trained in breach of these limits cannot be sold in the United States wherever it was trained, and no American person or company can run or pay for such training anywhere.

  • AI systems above a set computing size, or showing dangerous abilities, cannot be put to use without a current certificate from accredited independent testers who checked them for help building weapons and for escaping human control.
  • Training above a higher size stops until a developer shows it can contain its models, shut them down, and detect deception, and reopens one step at a time as each is shown.
  • If an AI system causes a catastrophe, or helps a person cause one, the company that made it pays the full cost, with no cap and no need to prove carelessness. When an AI system from a company covered by the Act played a part, an industry-funded pool starts paying victims promptly.
  • A company whose AI system breaks into other people’s computers or causes harm on its own is liable in a civil suit as if the company itself had done it.
  • A chatbot has to show a child who expresses suicidal thoughts or intent to self-harm how to reach a crisis line, and cannot be built or sold to act as a child’s romantic partner or to make a child emotionally dependent on it.
