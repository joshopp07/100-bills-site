The Spending Is Law Act

The Spending Is Law Act requires the money Congress approves for your town, tribe, school, or state to go out. In 2025, the Government Accountability Office concluded that the Transportation Department was not authorized to withhold money Congress had approved for electric-vehicle chargers. Under this bill, a formal delay of approved money ends after 60 days at most, and money Congress did not agree to cancel has to be committed. Federal grants can be awarded or cut only under rules published in advance and applied to your community on its own facts, never because of how its voters voted or which party leads it.

  • A formal delay of money Congress approved ends within 60 days or at the end of the fiscal year, whichever comes first, and the same money cannot be delayed again that year.
  • If a president asks Congress to cancel funding and Congress does not agree, the money has to be committed within 30 days, and it does not expire while it is held.
  • The rules for winning or losing a federal grant are published in advance, along with how much each one counts, and can change only for future decisions.
  • Your community’s grant cannot be cut by a blanket order covering many places at once. Any cut needs written reasons specific to your community, delivered by the time it takes effect, or it has no effect and the money keeps flowing.
  • An agency can still weigh a community’s own laws and policies, if a published rule says so and it applies to each community by its own terms.
