The Ports of Entry Act

The Ports of Entry Act puts a scanner and a trained officer in front of every vehicle coming through the busy border crossings, so that fentanyl, other drugs, and weapons are caught where smugglers carry them. By a date set in law, each vehicle gets a scan or, if the driver prefers, a hand search, including at the crossings today’s plans leave out. An officer reads every scan, and software can point out where to look but can never by itself justify a search. A crossing that falls short gets more officer positions, wait times are published for each crossing, and guns and bulk cash heading south are inspected too.

  • By a date set in law, every vehicle entering at a busy crossing gets a scan or a hand search, including at the crossings today’s plans leave out.
  • An officer reads every scan before seeing any software flag, and a flag alone can never justify a search.
  • A crossing that falls short of its standards gets more officer positions, and an officer moved away from the crossing counts as a vacancy to fill.
  • Wait times at each crossing are published, and longer lines bring more officer positions, not fewer inspections.
  • Guns and bulk cash heading south are inspected too.
