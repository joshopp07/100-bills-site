Methodology

This package was built by Josh Oppenheimer, using Claude Opus 4.8, in July 2026.

The drafting method combined three things: constraint-satisfaction design, where each provision was required to survive a fixed set of legal and political constraints simultaneously rather than being optimized for any one of them; mock arbitration among AI-generated agents mimicking political, economic, and legal public figures, used to pressure-test provisions from the vantage point and known priorities of real-world perspectives before a real critic could raise the same objection; and red-team / blue-team prompting, in which every mechanism was first attacked for its game-theoretic, constitutional, and political failure modes, then answered with a design change or explicitly conceded where no fix existed.

What this means and doesn't mean

The mock-arbitration exercises are an internal drafting tool, not a record of endorsement. No real person named or evoked in that process reviewed, endorsed, or was consulted about any provision of these bills. Positions generated through that process were used only to sharpen the drafting; they are not attributed to any individual anywhere in the operative text or the companion rationale.

Legal currency

This package reflects constitutional law current through the end of October Term 2025. Where a legal question is genuinely unsettled rather than resolved by existing precedent, the companion notes say so explicitly, along with what severability does if a provision is struck down.
