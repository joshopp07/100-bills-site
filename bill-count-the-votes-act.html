The Count the Votes Act

The Count the Votes Act makes every vote in a federal election leave a paper ballot the voter can check. After every federal election, officials hand-check a random sample of those ballots, large enough to catch a wrong winner, and publish what they find. If the hand check finds that the machine count named the wrong winner, the hand count replaces it as the official result. Skipping elections can never by itself start the process of taking you off the voter rolls, and before being removed, you get a personal notice and a chance to fix any mistake.

  • Every vote is counted from words on paper the voter can read, not from a code.
  • If the hand check of paper ballots shows the machines got the winner wrong, the hand count becomes the official result.
  • Skipping elections can never by itself start the process of taking you off the voter rolls.
  • Someone applying for a federal benefit is offered the chance to register to vote.
  • States that agree to award their electoral votes to the national popular vote winner have Congress’s consent.
