The Patient Safety and Compensation Act

Studies of hospital records have found that only a small share of patients injured by negligent care ever sue. This bill gives people injured by avoidable care a way to be paid without a lawsuit. You file a free claim with a federal program and do not need to prove anyone was at fault. If your injury qualifies, the program pays for the care you need, replaces your lost earnings, and adds a set amount for pain and suffering. No lawyer can take a percentage of it. You file here first, and if you turn down the offer, you can still sue.

  • The program publishes how each paid injury happened and what would have prevented it, with patient and clinician names removed, so hospitals and clinicians can learn from it.
  • At the start, a claim qualifies if an avoidable injury disabled you for six months or left a permanent impairment of at least 10 percent. The bill bars raising that threshold, which falls over time to an injury that disables you for four days or any permanent impairment.
  • If your injury keeps you from working, you are paid your lost take-home pay, with no maximum, and all of your compensation is free of income tax and does not count against Medicaid or any other federally funded benefit.
  • Your payment for pain and suffering arrives within 30 days of the decision and is the same for the same injury in every state, whatever your income, job, or age.
  • A claim is decided within 240 days by a panel of three: a member of the public who works for no hospital or insurer, and two doctors, one of them in the same specialty as the treating clinician. If the deadline passes, the claim is paid as filed. You can ask a new panel to look again for free, or take the claim to a patient-injury court, where the program pays your lawyer if your claim was made in good faith.
