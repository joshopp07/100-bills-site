The Puerto Rico Status Act

Puerto Rico voted on its status in 2012, 2017, 2020, and 2024, and it is still a territory. The Puerto Rico Status Act puts the island’s future to its voters in one election, on a ballot stating how long each choice would last and how it could change. If statehood wins, residents gain voting members of Congress, a vote for President, and federal benefits on the same terms as other states, while federal income tax phases in over ten years. If independence or free association wins, U.S. citizens keep their citizenship for life. Under any of those three outcomes, the federal financial oversight board ends, and its duties return to Puerto Rico’s government.

  • If statehood wins, federal income tax on Puerto Rico income phases in over ten years, while working families qualify for the federal earned income credit on the same terms as families in other states from the first tax year after admission.
  • If statehood wins, every eligible household is entitled to federal food assistance instead of drawing on a capped grant, and households already receiving aid do not have to reapply.
  • If statehood wins, residents eligible for Medicare are enrolled in Part B automatically, and those with low incomes can get the federal help with prescription drug costs available in other states.
  • If statehood wins, federal Medicaid money for Puerto Rico is no longer capped, and the federal share follows the same income formula as every other state’s, starting no lower than Puerto Rico’s share before admission. These terms end if the One Medicare Act replaces Medicaid, and Puerto Rico’s residents are then covered on the same terms as residents of every state.
  • If independence wins, people already receiving Social Security, veterans’ benefits, or federal retirement keep receiving them without interruption.
