The Public Option Act

Under the Public Option Act, choosing a private Medicare plan does not mean less coverage. Every private plan has to cover everything Medicare’s public plan covers, with no extra approval or referral the public plan does not use, and any doctor or hospital that takes Medicare has to take members of every private plan. A person sees in dollars what a plan costs beyond what Medicare pays before choosing it, and keeps the savings when a plan costs less. Medicare pays a private plan what its public plan spends on a comparable member.

  • A private Medicare plan has to cover every benefit the public plan covers and cannot swap in a lesser benefit or add an approval, a referral, or any other hurdle the public plan does not use.
  • Any doctor, hospital, or other provider that takes Medicare has to take patients in every private Medicare plan on the same terms, and cannot accept the public plan’s patients while turning away a private plan’s.
  • If a private plan costs less than traditional Medicare, a person choosing it gets the whole difference as a lower premium. Once the premium reaches zero, the rest comes as cash or an investment-account deposit.
  • If a private plan costs more than traditional Medicare, a person choosing it pays the difference as a higher premium, shown in dollars beside the plan, with no income-based help toward it.
  • A person comparing plans sees each plan’s results on members’ health and satisfaction, traditional Medicare’s included, beside its price.
