The Honest Price Act

The Honest Price Act makes the price you see include every fee the seller requires, and makes leaving a deal as easy as getting into it. Hotel rooms, concert tickets, and delivery apps show those fees in the first price you see, and a checkout page cannot pre-check a paid add-on. An unpaid medical bill stays off your credit report. Fine print can no longer send you to a faraway court, make you give up a jury, or limit what the law lets you recover.

  • Before you open a bank account, credit card, phone or internet plan, or subscription whose cost depends on how you use it, you see what the average customer paid last year and the amount that one customer in ten paid more than.
  • When a trial or introductory rate ends, or a subscription’s price goes up, the company has to warn you first and get your yes before charging the new amount.
  • A bank has to ask before charging you for overdraft coverage, cannot treat you worse for saying no, and has to show the dollar cost of its overdraft loans on your statement.
  • If a company charges you a fee this law forbids, you can send it a written demand, on a one-page federal form or in your own words, with no lawyer. If it does not pay within 30 days, it owes you the charge back plus $500.
  • Once a court or a regulator’s settlement finds a fee illegal, the company refunds every customer it charged in the last three years, without anyone having to ask, and the law requires it to keep the records that show who.
  • Every credit score comes with its math. The company publishes its formula, and you can see online, anytime and free, your score, how each item in your file raised or lowered it, and what it would be if you paid off 10 percent, 20 percent, and on up to all of what you owe. A lender, landlord, or employer with a minimum score has to print it on the application before you pay a fee.
