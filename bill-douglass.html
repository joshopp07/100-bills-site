The Douglass Commonwealth Admission Act

On the 2020 census, more people lived in Washington, D.C., than in Wyoming or Vermont, yet Congress can overturn the laws their local government passes. The Douglass Commonwealth Admission Act makes the city’s neighborhoods a state, equal to every other state, while a small federal Capital around the National Mall, the White House, the Capitol, and the Supreme Court remains the seat of the Federal Government separate from the Douglass Commonwealth. Residents make their own laws through their own elected government. Existing laws, officials, and court cases carry over. Statehood begins about a year after the bill becomes law.

  • Congress can no longer overturn the laws the new state’s elected government passes, or hold them for review before they take effect.
  • The new state’s Governor, rather than the President, commands its National Guard, and the President can call it into federal service on the same terms as any other state’s.
  • Every federal right, benefit, and protection that a state’s residents receive reaches the new state’s residents, whether or not other laws are rewritten to name it.
  • The new state can decide how tall its buildings may be, except that it cannot allow taller ones in the blocks around the federal Capital.
  • Federal employees who move to the new state’s government with the courts, prosecution, prisons, or parole keep their federal retirement and health benefits.
