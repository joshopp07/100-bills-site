The Minimum Wage Act

The Minimum Wage Act raises the federal minimum wage from today’s $7.25 in steps to $17 an hour five years after the law takes effect, and after that rises each year with middle-class wages. To protect jobs, each step and each later raise takes effect in a state only while the minimum stays below a set share of that state’s median wage. Once a step takes effect, the minimum can never fall. The bill also ends the lower wages federal law now allows for workers with disabilities and for teenagers in their first 90 days on a job, and it raises the cash wage paid to people who work for tips.

  • The minimum starts at $9.50 and reaches $17 an hour in five raises of $1.50 each, with automatic rises tied to middle-class wages after that.
  • If you work for tips, your cash wage is a fixed share of the minimum, so it rises whenever the minimum rises. You have the right to keep your tips, no employer or manager can take any of them, and you can still share them with the kitchen staff.
  • Sixteen states had passed laws ending below-minimum pay for workers with disabilities as of January 2025, and this bill brings those workers nationwide to the full minimum over five years, with transition help and no new federal certificates.
  • The $4.25 wage employers may now pay new hires under 20 for their first 90 days rises until it meets the full minimum.
  • Pay floors for contractors and app workers, which other bills in this family tie to the minimum wage, rise with it.
