The Gun Trafficking Act

The Gun Trafficking Act closes the routes that put guns in dangerous hands. Buying a handgun works like getting a driver’s license: pass a background check and a safety course, and the permit lasts five years. Private gun sales go through the same background check as a store sale. Gun kits carry serial numbers, and a device that makes a gun fire like a machine gun is treated as one. A conviction for abusing a dating partner bars having guns on the same terms as abusing a spouse. No one needs a permit to keep a handgun already owned or has to register a gun already owned.

  • A handgun buyer needs a five-year permit, open to adults 21 and older and to service members and police officers from 18 who pass a background check and a safety course, with a fee of no more than $75, and waits 72 hours after applying to buy from a dealer.
  • A private gun sale, whether at a gun show, online, or between acquaintances, runs through a licensed dealer’s background check for a fee of no more than $35, and no sale through a dealer goes through while the check is pending.
  • A gun kit that can readily be finished into a working gun gets a serial number and a background check at sale, and any device that makes a gun fire like a machine gun is treated as a machine gun, whatever its maker calls it.
  • If police, a family or household member, or a clinician who has treated a person shows a judge that the person poses a significant risk of killing or seriously injuring someone, including themselves, the person’s guns go to a dealer, a police agency, or someone outside the home to hold them for safekeeping. The order ends on its own within a year unless the danger is shown again, and the person can then ask for the guns back. The person can also ask the judge, once during each order, to end it early, and the judge ends it unless the danger is shown again.
  • Magazines holding more than ten rounds can no longer be made, imported, or sold, and dealers can sell semiautomatic rifles and shotguns only to buyers 21 and older, while every magazine already owned, and an 18-year-old’s use of a rifle for hunting or sport, is untouched.
