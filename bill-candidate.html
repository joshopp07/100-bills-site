The Candidate Integrity Act

The Candidate Integrity Act keeps the money people give to campaigns from turning into a payday for the candidate or the candidate’s family. A campaign cannot pay a company the candidate or a relative has a stake in, even at a fair price, and a relative on the payroll has to do real work and be paid no more than the going rate. Inaugural committees, legal defense funds, and presidential library foundations make their donors public, and they and leadership PACs follow the same rules. A candidate cannot collect a consulting fee from their own campaign or rent a building to their own campaign.

  • All the money from campaign merchandise bearing the candidate’s name, face, or brand stays with the campaign or group that sold it, and none of it can go to the candidate or the family.
  • A candidate, the campaign’s staff, and its paid consultants cannot place a bet on the candidate’s race on a prediction market, whether the bet wins on a victory or a loss.
  • When a campaign or political committee pays someone to post about a candidate, the post itself says so and names who paid.
  • An account a political group runs to post about candidates names the group in its profile, and a group that buys an account has to disclose the purchase.
  • A legal defense fund can pay only outside lawyers, directly, and none of its money can go to the candidate or the family.
