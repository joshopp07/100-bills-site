The Permitting Act

The Permitting Act puts federal environmental reviews of new homes, transit lines, water and sewer systems, and power lines on a deadline. Each review covers the effects the agency has power to act on, and the agency cannot skip them. A home or apartment project on an already-developed lot in town no longer waits on a federal study at all. AI can help draft an environmental study, but cannot decide that a study is not needed. A permit once issued can be stopped only by a court, for breach or fraud, or for up to 30 days on a written finding of imminent danger.

  • A home or apartment project on an already-developed lot in town no longer waits on a federal environmental study because federal money or a permit touched it, and the laws protecting wetlands, floodplains, and historic places still apply.
  • A transit line, water or sewer project, or power line built on land already in use gets its federal sign-off without a long study, unless something specific about that site calls for one.
  • If a federal agency misses its deadline for an environmental study, the builder’s own public study can move the project forward, and anyone can still challenge it in court. If an agency misses its decision date on a permit, the permit issues under every rule that already applies.
  • A permit once issued can be stopped only by a court, for breach or fraud, or for up to 30 days on a written finding of imminent danger, so the next President cannot cancel a project halfway through.
  • You see the draft of any full environmental study and get at least 45 days to comment before a federal agency decides, whichever agency it is.
