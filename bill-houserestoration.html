The People's House Act

For more than a century, the law has set the House at 435 seats, and the average number of people per Representative has grown to about 761,000. The People’s House Act replaces the fixed number with a rule that lets the House grow, so most Representatives answer to fewer people. After each census, the number of seats follows from the count. On the 2020 figures, the House has 600 members, with an average of about 552,000 people per Representative. The new rule begins with the seats apportioned after the 2030 census, and no sitting member’s term is cut short.

  • On the 2020 count, the most people per Representative in any state falls from about 991,000 to about 578,000, and the number falls in 48 of the 50 states.
  • Every state has at least two Representatives, so no state’s whole House delegation is one person.
  • The number of seats is no longer fixed, so one state’s gain is not always another state’s loss. After each census, the population of the smallest state becomes the reference point: each state’s population is divided by it and rounded up, and every state gets at least two Representatives. For example, after the 2020 census, Wyoming was the smallest state, with about 578,000 residents, and California was the largest, with about 39,577,000. Under the People’s House Act, Wyoming would have two Representatives (578,000 ÷ 578,000 = 1, raised to the minimum of two), and California would have 69 (39,577,000 ÷ 578,000 = 68.5, rounded up to 69).
  • A new census can make the House smaller only if the population of the country as a whole falls.
  • On the 2020 count, the Electoral College has 704 electors, and electoral votes across the states track population more closely overall than they do today.
