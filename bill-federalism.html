The State Budget Relief Act

Together with the One Medicare Act, which ends the state share of Medicaid, the State Budget Relief Act keeps the cost of health coverage for low-income residents off your state’s budget. The money your state spends on Medicaid now is its own to use on schools, roads, or lower taxes. Federal income taxes rise under the Individual Tax Fairness Act to pay for the One Medicare Act’s coverage. In return, your state takes over highway, job-training, school, and community-development programs and sets their priorities. In every year of the ten-year transition, the Treasury pays any state that comes out behind, counting the gas-tax room it can collect whether or not it does.

  • The federal government pays for Medicaid.
  • The federal gas tax comes down in steps as highway programs move to your state over five years, and your state can collect the same amount itself by a gas tax, a road-usage charge, or another tax, so drivers see no net change if it does.
  • Job-training, school, and community-development money comes to your state as block grants for ten years, and your state decides how to spend it.
  • Title I aid for low-income schools and special-education funding for students with disabilities do not change, and this law can never be read to cut either.
  • National rules for Interstate condition and transportation safety, freight corridors of national significance, public transit programs, civil-rights enforcement in schools, veterans’ employment services, Job Corps, and disaster-recovery aid all stay federal.
