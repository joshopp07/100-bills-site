The Medical Care PAID Act

The Medical Care Protection Against Insurance Denials (PAID) Act makes health plans deliver the care they sell. A clinician in the same or a similar specialty has to make any decision that a treatment is medically unnecessary, and a request a plan leaves unanswered past its deadline is approved. A patient who sees a doctor the plan’s directory wrongly lists pays no more than in-network prices. A job-based plan that carelessly denied care owes the full cost of the harm. An employer that pays toward anyone’s coverage has to offer part-time workers the same plan, and copay coupons from drugmakers count toward a patient’s deductible.

  • A decision that a treatment is medically unnecessary has to come from a clinician in the same or a similar specialty, and a request a plan leaves unanswered past its deadline is approved.
  • A patient who sees a doctor listed in the plan’s directory who is not actually taking the plan’s new patients pays no more than in-network prices, and that amount counts toward the deductible and out-of-pocket limit.
  • A plan cannot limit mental health or addiction care unless its network gives patients access to that care comparable to its access for medical and surgical care.
  • A job-based plan that carelessly denied or delayed a benefit owes the benefit, interest, and the full cost of the harm the denial or delay caused, including injury or death.
  • An employer that pays toward health coverage for any worker has to offer part-time employees the same plan at the same share of the cost, prorated for their hours.
