The Veterans and Military Care Act

Of the 213,580 veterans who left service in 2023, 77 died by suicide within 12 months, according to the VA. This bill closes the gap service members fall into when they move from military health care to VA care. If you qualify, you are enrolled in VA health care automatically, your medical record follows you, and any treatment underway continues for at least your first year out. Where military and VA facilities serve the same area, they share one management, one medical record, and one clinical staff, while neither department merges into the other, no facility closes, and no one’s benefits change.

  • The record system both departments are already installing becomes a legal duty: your military and VA clinicians work from one shared record, with no transfer or request needed.
  • Any course of treatment underway when you leave the service continues on the same terms for at least 12 months, and a dispute over which system pays can never delay your care.
  • If you are in mental health care and miss your first appointment after leaving the service, someone reaches out within one business day.
  • Doctors, nurses, and other clinicians are expected to rotate between military and VA hospitals where distance allows, and a credential earned in one system is honored in the other.
  • This bill does not change your VA eligibility or benefits, reduce any service member’s or family’s TRICARE coverage, or privatize any part of either system.
