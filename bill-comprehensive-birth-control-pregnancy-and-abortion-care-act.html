The Comprehensive Birth Control, Pregnancy and Abortion Care Act

The Comprehensive Birth Control, Pregnancy and Abortion Care Act makes abortion lawful in every state before a pregnancy reaches viability, by pills or by surgery. After viability, care stays protected when a clinician judges in good faith that it is needed to protect the patient’s life or health, including mental health. No state can make a patient wait, make an extra trip, or hear information outside medical guidelines, or hold abortion clinics to building and staffing rules it does not apply to comparable care. A clinician licensed in any state can prescribe abortion pills by telehealth, the pills can be mailed to a patient in any state, and no government can punish the patient for using them.

  • No state can ban abortion, by pills or by surgery, before a pregnancy reaches viability.
  • After viability, a patient can get an abortion when a clinician judges in good faith that it is needed to protect the patient’s life or health, and health includes mental health.
  • Medicare covers birth control with no copay or deductible, kept confidential, and abortion care on the same terms as any other care, and limits on federal funding do not apply to Medicare.
  • Medicare covers pregnancy without a break from the day it is confirmed until twelve months after it ends, with no premium, deductible, or copay, including mental health care, addiction care, and a doula’s support.
  • The 1873 Comstock law can no longer be used to stop abortion pills, or information about abortion, from moving through the mail.
