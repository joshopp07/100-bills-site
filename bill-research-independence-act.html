The Research Independence Act

The Research Independence Act keeps politics out of federal science. Once a grant is awarded, the agency can cancel it only for a cause the law lists, never because its priorities changed. Political appointees cannot score science grants, and scientists can publish their own findings without political clearance. Scientists spend less time on forms, and more research money goes to long awards to scientists instead of single projects. Anyone can read the research the public pays for and check its data.

  • A dispute with a university cannot cancel every grant at that university; each grant termination needs its own cause.
  • No political appointee can review or score a science grant, and any refusal of a grant that reviewers recommended has to state its legal reason, in public.
  • Scientists applying for federal grants send only their scientific proposals first, and the budget and other paperwork in an application wait until a proposal scores well enough to fund.
  • More research money goes to long awards for scientists instead of short ones for single projects, and a share of those awards go to scientists who have never held a major grant.
  • A federal scientist can publish and discuss their own research without political clearance.
