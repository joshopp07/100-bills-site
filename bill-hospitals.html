The Keeping Hospitals Open Act

The Keeping Hospitals Open Act pays hospitals each month to keep an emergency room, a delivery unit, and trauma care ready at all hours, and a hospital drawing that payment cannot add separate facility fees to outpatient bills. Medicare stops paying more for a routine outpatient service just because a hospital owns the clinic. A nonprofit hospital keeps its tax exemption only by giving free care to patients with household income up to twice the poverty line, whatever their savings, and discounts on care for patients with household income up to four times the poverty line. A hospital cannot charge a patient or an insurer more for a drug or supply than what it paid plus a set markup and a handling fee.

  • A qualifying hospital is paid each month to keep its emergency room, a labor and delivery unit that can perform a cesarean at any hour, and its trauma care ready at all hours, and cannot charge a separate facility fee for a covered outpatient service while it draws that payment.
  • Medicare pays the same for a routine outpatient service at a hospital-owned clinic as at a doctor’s office.
  • A person who is uninsured, or whose copay would be higher, pays a hospital or clinic that buys a drug at the federal 340B discount no more than that discounted price plus a dispensing fee.
  • A nonprofit hospital cannot sue a patient, sell the debt, or report it to credit bureaus before deciding whether the patient qualifies for free or discounted care, and enrollment in a program the law lists qualifies a person for free care with no separate application.
  • New Medicare-supported training positions for doctors go to areas in proportion to their measured shortage of doctors, and a clinician working in an area short of health professionals can get relief from student debt that leaving later does not take back.
