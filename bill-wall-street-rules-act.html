The Wall Street Rules Act

In the spring of 2023, Silicon Valley Bank, Signature Bank, and First Republic, each with more than $100 billion in assets, failed within two months. A 2018 law had raised the line for the strictest federal oversight of banks from $50 billion in assets to $250 billion. The Wall Street Rules Act brings that oversight back to banks above $50 billion. The rules that keep one company’s failure from reaching ordinary people apply to whoever carries the risk or holds the money, whatever its charter. Money in a payment app, and a federal benefit paid into one, get the protection a bank customer gets.

  • Money you keep in a payment app is held in full for you, and the app’s creditors cannot touch it if it fails.
  • Social Security or another federal benefit sitting in a payment app gets the same automatic protection from garnishment it gets at a bank.
  • Large life insurers have to hold more capital against private equity and private credit, and show what is in those holdings, so your annuity does not ride on assets nobody can see.
  • A trading app has to limit how much you can borrow to bet on a price, can never let you lose more than you put in, and has to show what share of its customers lose money.
