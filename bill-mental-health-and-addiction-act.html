The Mental Health and Addiction Act

Someone in a mental health crisis should reach help, and someone with an opioid addiction should be able to get the treatment that keeps them alive. The Mental Health and Addiction Act lets an addiction specialist prescribe methadone that a patient picks up at a pharmacy, not only at a specialized clinic. It funds the 988 suicide and crisis line permanently, so a call or text is more likely to reach a counselor in the caller’s own state. Health plans have to cover crisis care on the same terms as other care, and a plan that makes mental health care harder to get than medical care fails a yes-or-no test.

  • Methadone for opioid addiction can be prescribed by an addiction specialist and picked up at a pharmacy.
  • The 988 suicide and crisis line has permanent funding, tied to how many calls are answered in the caller’s own state.
  • Health plans have to cover crisis services, including a crisis team, on the same terms as other care.
  • A health plan that makes mental health or addiction care harder to get than other care fails a yes-or-no test, and until it passes, its members can see an out-of-network clinician at the in-network price.
  • Naloxone has permanent federal funding for distribution, and fentanyl test strips are legal under federal law.
