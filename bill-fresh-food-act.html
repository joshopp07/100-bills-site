The Fresh Food Act

The Fresh Food Act makes fruits and vegetables easier to grow and easier to afford. Farmers can plant fruits or vegetables on base acres without losing federal payments. Federal commodity payments end on a schedule, and small farms that meet the new standards get a payment on what they sell instead. Fruit and vegetable growers can buy crop insurance covering their whole farm. Families on SNAP get money back on produce at the register, families on WIC get a fruit-and-vegetable amount guaranteed by law, and more schools serve free meals to every student, whatever the family’s income.

  • A farmer can plant fruits or vegetables on any share of the farm’s base acres without losing federal payments.
  • Part of what a family spends on fruits and vegetables with SNAP comes back onto its card at the register, with nothing to sign up for.
  • Families on WIC get a fruit-and-vegetable amount guaranteed by law that rises with prices.
  • Any school where at least a quarter of students are identified as low income can serve breakfast and lunch free to every student, with no forms, because the federal government pays the free-meal rate for every meal, and a drop in a school’s share does not take it out for four years.
