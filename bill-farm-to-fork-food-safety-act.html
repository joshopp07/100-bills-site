The Farm to Fork Food Safety Act

The Farm to Fork Food Safety Act rests on one rule: the company that profits from a food does not get to certify that it is safe. No company can put a new ingredient into food on its own say-so, and ingredients already in food on a company’s own finding are checked again. The Food and Drug Administration has to set binding limits on lead, arsenic, and other contaminants in food, on deadlines written into law. Farms above a set size are inspected without warning, and a farm that fails cannot ship until it passes.

  • The FDA has to set binding limits on lead, arsenic, and other contaminants in all food, including baby food, on deadlines written into law.
  • No new ingredient can go into your food on a company’s own say-so that it is safe, and the experts vouching for it, with any financial ties they have to the company, are named in public.
  • Ingredients already in food on a company’s own safety finding have to be filed for review on a schedule or come off the market, and ingredients are reviewed again on a regular cycle.
  • Farms above a set size are inspected without warning, farms abroad that sell food here are inspected as often, and a farm that fails cannot ship until it passes.
  • Honey has to be pure honey, olive oil has to meet the grade on its label, and fish has to be the species its label names.
