The Medical Treatment During Pregnancy Act

In 2023, while a court order paused Idaho’s abortion ban for medical emergencies, St. Luke’s hospitals reported sending one pregnant patient out of state for an emergency termination. In the first three months after the pause was lifted in 2024, they reported airlifting six. A court order now protects only St. Luke’s, which runs 8 of Idaho’s 39 hospitals that accept Medicare. This bill protects pregnant patients whose lives or physical health are at serious risk, and their clinicians, in every state. When two doctors agree in writing, a patient can receive an abortion, or a treatment such as chemotherapy that endangers the pregnancy, without waiting to become critically ill. It stands even if the broader abortion bill fails.

  • A serious risk qualifies even if it is not yet immediate, including one arising from a condition such as a history of preeclampsia or diabetes.
  • Hospital emergency rooms have to provide an abortion when it is the treatment that stabilizes the patient, whatever state law says.
  • The patient can never be charged, sued, or penalized in any way for receiving care covered by a written finding.
  • A specialist can be reached by phone or video at any hour, with at least 90 percent of calls connected within 10 minutes, and no hospital, insurer, or state can require sign-off from a lawyer, committee, or administrator first.
  • Abortion pills prescribed for this care can be dispensed and mailed in any state on the terms the Food and Drug Administration approved, and a federal law from 1873 can no longer be used to treat them as illegal to mail.
