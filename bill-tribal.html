The Tribal Health Act

The Government Accountability Office found that in 2017 the Indian Health Service spent $4,078 per person served, compared with $13,185 for Medicare, whose enrollees are older. This bill puts Indian health care on permanent, full funding and expands tribes’ control of their own care. This bill treats Indian health care as an obligation the United States took on by treaty and statute, and nothing in it ends or reduces the Indian health system. Tribes that run their own programs have their full support costs paid, and clinics serving American Indian and Alaska Native patients keep their billing revenue on top of their funding.

  • Clinics run by the Indian Health Service, by tribes, and by urban Indian organizations can bill for covered patients at rates no lower than they receive today, and that revenue adds to their funding rather than reducing it, with urban Indian organizations on the same billing, payment, and program terms as tribal health programs.
  • A tribe’s application to run a health program itself is approved automatically if no answer comes within 90 days, which current rules already provide for contract proposals, with no restart for information requests, and the full funding for that program moves with it.
  • Traditional healing provided through an Indian health program is covered, with each tribe deciding what counts and no federal official able to require disclosure of sacred practice.
  • The Special Diabetes Program for Indians becomes permanent at no less than its current level, and a permanent fund builds and modernizes clinics and sanitation facilities based on condition and need.
  • The billing, urban parity, traditional healing, diabetes, and referral provisions begin when universal coverage under the One Medicare Act begins, and depend on that Act.
