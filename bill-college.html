The College Price Before Signing Day Act

The College Price Before Signing Day Act lets you see and compare what college will cost before you commit, and helps you keep the credits you already paid for. Any figure a college gives a rankings publisher has to match the figure it reports to the federal government. A college paying the tax on large endowments owes a lower rate when it cuts what federally aided students from families below the national median income actually pay and keeps them as large a share of its students.

  • A college’s aid offer has to show the full cost of the year, each grant and whether it renews, each loan labeled as a loan with its interest rate and fees, work-study labeled as a chance to earn wages, and the net price, so two offers can be compared side by side.
  • If you leave college partway through a term, the federal aid you have earned matches the share of the term you attended, all the way to the last day, and the loan money your college sends back comes off what you owe.
  • A 2025 law ends federal student loans for any college program whose graduates earn too little. This bill repeals that cutoff and instead limits federal borrowing for each program to what its graduates’ earnings can repay, and your aid offer shows that limit and the graduates’ earnings beside the same figures for other programs in the same field.
  • A student loan from a private bank can be discharged in bankruptcy, and every federal student loan stays as it is.
  • Next to its net price estimate, a college shows what it spends per student on teaching and, separately, on student services and on the businesses it runs on campus.
