The Fair Energy Market Act

The Fair Energy Market Act ends special deals under which the public pays costs that belong to the oil, gas, and coal industries. Oil and gas companies pay for the federal programs that regulate them, and a new well on federal land needs a bond covering the estimated full cost of plugging it. Producers pay a fee on every barrel, and crews are hired to plug abandoned wells. The federal government becomes a steady customer for steel, cement, and glass as clean as the cleanest quarter of American production, and new federal vehicles move to zero-emission class by class.

  • Companies that drill, mine, and pipe fossil fuels pay fees covering about the full cost of five named federal regulators, including the share taxpayers pay now, since those regulators exist only to oversee those industries.
  • No company can drill a new well on federal land without first posting a bond covering the estimated full cost of plugging it and cleaning up the site, and a company that sells a well stays responsible until the buyer posts one.
  • Oil and gas producers pay a fee on every barrel and every thousand cubic feet of gas, and crews are hired to plug abandoned wells, with a preference for people who live in the region or lost jobs in it.
  • The methane charge already in law starts the year after the bill is enacted instead of in 2034.
