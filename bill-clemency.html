The Pardon Transparency Act

A President can still pardon anyone under the Pardon Transparency Act, but the public sees every pardon and commutation. Each one goes on a public list showing the person, the offense, and the date. The government has to notify the victims of the crime. A pardon leaves a court order to repay those victims in force unless the pardon says it cancels that order and names it. No taxpayer money can pay a pardoned person compensation or legal fees for the conduct the pardon covered.

  • Each pardon or commutation on the public list shows whether it went through the Justice Department’s review, whether the Department made a recommendation, and whether the President followed it.
  • The government has to notify the victims of a pardoned crime no later than the day the pardon goes on the public list.
  • A pardon leaves a forfeiture of property in force unless the pardon says it cancels the forfeiture and names it.
  • No federal money can pay a pardoned person compensation, an indemnity, a settlement, or legal fees for the conduct the pardon covered.
