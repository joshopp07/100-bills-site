The Supreme Court Terms Amendment

The Supreme Court Terms Amendment writes a fixed schedule for the Supreme Court into the Constitution. Justices serve eighteen-year terms on a Court of nine, then become Senior Justices who keep the office, the title, and the full salary. The Justices serving on the day it is ratified move to senior status one at a time, every two years, longest-serving first. The Judiciary Integrity Act sets the same schedule in ordinary law, but the Amendment serves as a constitutional backstop.

  • No Congress can add a seat to the Supreme Court or take one away, because the Constitution fixes the active bench at nine.
  • A Justice’s death or early departure does not move the schedule, because the replacement serves only the rest of that eighteen-year term.
  • The job of Chief Justice passes every two years to the Justice in the middle two years of an eighteen-year term, with no separate nomination, no confirmation vote, and no extra pay.
