The American Grid Act

The American Grid Act gets new power onto the grid sooner and keeps the added cost of the wires and capacity that giant new data centers need off your bill. A power project, including solar, wind, and batteries, can plug in faster if it accepts being cut back when the lines are full, and a power line crossing state lines can get one federal permit. You can pay for a heat pump, solar panels, or a battery on your electric bill with no credit check. You can share your own meter data with anyone you choose and run your home from your electric car. Data centers and other giant new power users match their power with clean energy.

  • A power project waiting to connect, including solar, wind, and batteries, can plug in without first waiting on a study of upgrades across the whole network, if it accepts being cut back when the lines are full.
  • If you sign up with a company that pays households for cutting their use when the grid is strained, your utility cannot refuse you service or change your rates over that choice.
  • Your electric car can power your home with no permit, fee, meter change, or notice to your utility, as long as the equipment cannot send power back onto the grid.
  • A new data center or other giant new power user has to turn in clean energy credits equal to all of the electricity it uses each year, and a plant burning carbon-based fuel to serve one has to shut down or convert by a set date.
  • A giant data center cannot make more noise at the nearest home than a limit set in law, day or night, measured rather than estimated, and it has to meet a limit on its cooling water use.
