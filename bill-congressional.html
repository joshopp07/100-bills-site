The Congressional Integrity Act

The Congressional Integrity Act applies the rules of the [Executive Integrity Act](law.html?bill=executive) to members of Congress and their senior staff, with each chamber enforcing its members’ recusals. Members and senior staff sit on no company’s board and take no fee for a speech. Stocks and similar investments have to be sold, handed to an independent trustee they cannot direct, or kept in public view while the member steps aside from every vote that could change the asset’s value and pays a yearly tax that climbs to 10 percent of its value.

  • A member’s salary rises only when middle-class earnings rise, and by the same percentage.
  • No member of Congress, congressional staffer, or other legislative-branch employee can bet on a prediction market, whether or not they know anything the public does not.
  • A member who knows before the public does what Congress or the government is about to do cannot hold a stock, an option, or a wager that pays off when it happens.
  • For five years after leaving, a former member can take no pay, board seat, or speaking fee from a company with significant business before a committee they served on.
  • A family member’s own working business never has to be sold, and a business owned before taking office can be held, with no role in running it, for up to four years before it must be sold.
