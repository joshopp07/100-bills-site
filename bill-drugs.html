The Lowest Drug Price Wins Act

The Lowest Drug Price Wins Act lowers what people pay for prescriptions under Medicare and makes the drugs themselves safer. Medicare negotiates prices on more drugs each year, including drugs for rare diseases, and a new drug launched at a high price can be negotiated in the next round with no waiting period. Every version of every generic drug Medicare pays for is tested by independent laboratories, and overseas plants making those drugs face inspections without warning. A pharmacy receives the full Medicare payment for a generic, with no middleman keeping a share.

  • Medicare negotiates prices on more drugs each year, including drugs for rare diseases and drugs from small biotech companies that current law leaves out.
  • A new drug launched above a set price threshold becomes eligible for negotiation in the next round, with no waiting period, and its maker refunds what Medicare paid above the negotiated price.
  • A drugmaker that raises a drug’s price faster than inflation owes a rebate on every unit dispensed to a person on Medicare.
  • Every version of every generic drug Medicare pays for is tested by independent laboratories. Waiting for a test does not delay anyone’s access, and one maker’s failure never takes other makers’ versions off coverage.
  • Overseas plants making drugs Medicare pays for are inspected without warning at least once every 24 months, and at least as often as comparable plants in the United States.
