The Floods and Fires Act

The Floods and Fires Act prepares communities for floods, wildfires, storm surge, and extreme heat before disaster strikes, so that less has to be rebuilt after it. Federal money stops paying for new homes in the areas at the top of the risk scale, while existing homes keep their support. For each place at risk, the government funds the least costly way to keep people safe, and protects a community in place when the cost is close to a buyout. Money to prepare for disasters reaches at-risk communities by a formula based on hazard and population, so a community’s share does not depend on winning a grant competition.

  • Where a buyout is the least costly way to keep a family safe, the owner gets a guaranteed offer at the home’s full pre-disaster value if it is their primary home, on their own timeline.
  • Federal money no longer pays for new construction in the areas at the top of the risk scale, and no existing home loses its support because of the change.
  • Flood insurance premiums on existing homes keep moving toward their full-risk price in the capped yearly steps current law sets, and the guaranteed buyout is the way out.
  • If you buy or rent a home, you are told its flood and fire risk and its flood-claims history before you sign.
