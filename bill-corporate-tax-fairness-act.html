The Corporate Tax Fairness Act

The Corporate Tax Fairness Act lowers the tax on hiring a person and makes it harder to move profits out of reach. Corporations pay one 28 percent rate, below the 35 percent rate in place before the 2017 tax law, while the profits of a privately held company are taxed at each owner’s own rate. Breaks that fix a measured problem can stay, and those written for one industry end. Every company gets the same delayed start date and no special transition relief.

  • Companies are encouraged to hire more workers by lowering payroll taxes.
  • Companies are discouraged from buying robots to replace workers by narrowing capital investment tax breaks.
  • The tax credits for research, which returns at least twice as much to society as to the company doing it, and for clean electricity and clean manufacturing can stay.
  • Companies that make money from general-purpose AI models pay a tax of at least 5 percent on their U.S. revenue from those models above $25 million a year, because the models were trained on the public’s knowledge.
