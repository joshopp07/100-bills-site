The Keep Your Benefits Act

The Keep Your Benefits Act lets benefit programs stop making you prove, again and again, income the government already knows about. After pandemic coverage rules ended, more than 25 million people were dropped from Medicaid, nearly seven in ten of them for paperwork or procedural reasons. The Treasury keeps a record of your income from wage and tax reports already filed, whether or not you have ever filed a return. Where it shows you qualify, participating programs, Medicaid among them, can use it to sign you up with no new application, which you can decline, and to renew you with no new paperwork. The record states facts only, and each program still applies its own rules.

  • A participating program has to try to renew you from the information it already has before asking you for anything, cannot require an in-person interview to renew you, and gives you at least 90 days to answer any request.
  • In tax year 2022, about one in five people eligible for the earned income tax credit did not claim it. If your income is all reported by employers and others, the IRS sends you a completed return each year with the credits the records support, and you can accept it, correct it, or ignore it without its counting against you.
  • A change in your records that raises your benefit takes effect as soon as it is detected, while one that lowers it waits until your next scheduled renewal.
  • If you are cut off for not returning a form and qualified all along, you can send it in within 90 days and have your benefit restored back to the day it ended, with no new application.
  • Income records shared with a program to run your benefits cannot be used for immigration enforcement.
