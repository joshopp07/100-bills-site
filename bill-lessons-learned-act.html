The Lessons Learned Act

The Lessons Learned Act makes the federal government study its own record and show the public what it finds. An independent board looks back at large federal programs on a fixed schedule that no party or President can choose. After every national emergency and every deadly disaster, it reports on what went right and what went wrong. It studies what worked as well as what failed, so that successes get copied. When the board recommends a change, the agency that could implement the change has 90 days to say publicly whether it will make it, and anyone can check whether it does.

  • An independent board looks back at large federal programs on a fixed schedule that no party or President can choose, and studies what worked as well as what failed.
  • After every national emergency and every deadly disaster, the board publishes a report on what went right and what went wrong.
  • When the board recommends a change, the agency has 90 days to say publicly whether it will make it and why, and anyone can check whether it does.
  • Every federal agency has to carry out the studies of its programs that its own plan promises, and publish every result.
  • A federal employee can tell the board what went wrong without risking their job over what they said.
