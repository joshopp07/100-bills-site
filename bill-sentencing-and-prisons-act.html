The Sentencing and Prisons Act

The Sentencing and Prisons Act makes federal drug sentences shorter. Mandatory minimums under the main federal drug-trafficking law fall, and crack cocaine is punished the same as powder, with anyone serving the longer crack sentence able to ask a judge for the new one. The safety valve that lets a judge go below a drug minimum reaches more people. Someone who has served 10 years of a longer federal sentence can ask a judge for a second look. Federal prisons get a required number of inspections each year, and nobody can lose property to federal forfeiture without a conviction. No one can be sentenced to death or executed under federal law.

  • Under the main federal drug-trafficking law, mandatory minimums of 10 and 5 years fall to 5 and 2, and crack cocaine is punished the same as powder.
  • A criminal record keeps a person from the safety valve, which lets a judge go below a drug minimum, only if it meets all three of the law’s disqualifying tests.
  • Someone who has served 10 years of a longer federal sentence can ask a judge for a second look, and the judge has to find they are no danger and ready to come home.
  • The federal prison inspections Congress ordered in 2024 get permanent funding and a required number each year.
  • Nobody can lose property to federal forfeiture without a conviction, and the money goes to the Treasury instead of the agency that seized it.
