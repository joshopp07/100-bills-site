The Workplace Safety and Inspection Act

A workplace safety inspection prevents injuries. In a randomized study of California workplaces, injury rates were 9.4 percent lower at inspected sites, with no significant difference in jobs, sales, or business survival. A later study found that better choices of where to inspect would have prevented as much as twice the injuries with the same number of inspections. The Workplace Safety and Inspection Act makes those inspections happen every year. If you see a hazard and can’t afford to be seen reporting it, a union or workers’ group can take the report to inspectors for you.

  • Federal inspectors reach at least 25,000 routine safety inspections a year on a schedule written into the law.
  • The rule for choosing where to inspect first applies both where federal inspectors work and in the 22 states and territories that run their own workplace safety programs.
  • No one can fire, discipline, or take work away from you because you told a union or workers’ group about conditions at your job or because your name appears in its report.
  • When an inspection follows a report from a registered union or workers’ group, a representative it chooses can walk through the workplace with the inspector, if a worker there agrees in writing.
  • A registered group that files a report can get the employer’s log of injuries and illnesses for that workplace, with injured workers’ names and other identifying information removed, by the end of the next business day.
